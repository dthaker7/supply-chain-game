📖 The Cafe — Complete Guide

🎯 Objective

You are the supply chain manager of The Cafe. Over 30 days, manage inventory for 2 products — Coffee and Cookies. Your goal is to keep shelves stocked, minimize costs, and maximize your service level. The game ends after Day 30 and you receive a grade (A+ to F).

🏆 Target: ≥90% service level + positive gross profit = Grade A or better

📦 Core Concept — Inventory Management

Each day customers arrive and buy products. If you don't have enough inventory, you lose sales (stockout). If you hold too much, you pay holding costs every day. The challenge is finding the balance.

  • On-hand inventory — units physically in your store right now
  • Pipeline / In-Transit — units you've ordered but haven't arrived yet. Count these when deciding whether to reorder!
  • Inventory Position = On-hand + Pipeline. This is what actually matters for ordering decisions.
  • Service Level = Total units sold ÷ Total customer demand. A stockout hurts this permanently.

📐 EOQ — Economic Order Quantity

EOQ is the mathematically optimal order size that minimizes total inventory cost (holding cost + ordering cost). Named after Ford Harris (1913).

EOQ = √( 2 × D × K / h )
  • D = average daily demand
  • K = fixed order cost (set by your instructor per product)
  • h = holding cost per unit per day

The game calculates EOQ for you and shows it on the Order tab. Order sizes near EOQ minimize your total cost over time.

🚚 Lead Time & Reorder Point (ROP)

When you place an order it doesn't arrive immediately — there's a lead time (1–3 days depending on supplier). You need to order before you run out.

ROP = Average Daily Demand × Lead Time

When your Inventory Position drops below the ROP, it's time to reorder. The game shows "ORDER NOW" on the overview when this happens.

💡 Always check Inventory Position (on-hand + pipeline), not just on-hand. You might already have stock on the way!

💰 Holding Costs — Why They Differ Per Product

Holding cost = cost of keeping 1 unit in stock for 1 day. It covers storage space, spoilage risk, and tied-up capital. Different products have different rates:

ProductHolding Cost/unit/dayWhy
☕ Coffee$0.10Medium — beans lose freshness
🍪 Cookies$0.06Low — packaged, shelf stable

Cookies are cheapest to hold → safe to over-stock. Coffee is more expensive → order closer to EOQ and avoid excess.

🏭 Suppliers — Trade-offs

Each product has 2 suppliers to choose from. You can switch between them any day before placing an order. Key trade-offs:

  • Cost vs Reliability — cheaper suppliers are often less reliable
  • Lead Time vs Price — express suppliers charge more but arrive faster
  • Supplier Blocking — some suppliers get blocked by events (factory pause, shipping crisis). Switching to the backup supplier is your escape.
💡 For critical products (coffee during a morning rush), use the premium reliable supplier. For stable products, the budget option saves money.

⚡ Events — Product-Specific Disruptions

Random events hit specific products on specific days. An upcoming event is shown at the top of the canvas. Types:

  • Demand Spike (×multiplier) — demand suddenly increases. Stock up before the event hits!
  • Supplier Delay (+X days) — your order takes longer. Order earlier or switch suppliers.
  • Supplier Fail — that supplier is blocked for N days. Switch immediately.
  • Price Change — selling price changes. Affects your revenue.
⚠️ Events are shown on the canvas banner up to 4 days ahead. Use this to pre-order before a spike hits!

🕹️ How to Play — Day by Day

  • 1. Check the Dashboard tab — see both products at a glance. "⚠ ORDER" badges mean you're below ROP.
  • 2. Use Quick Order — on each product card, choose your supplier, enter a quantity (or use the EOQ recommendation), and click Place Order.
  • 3. Repeat for each product you need to order. Orders are queued (shown with ✓ badge) until you advance.
  • 4. Click "Next Day →" — the day animation plays, customers arrive, sales happen, deliveries come in, holding costs are deducted.
  • 5. Check Costs tab — monitor holding costs, ordering costs, and profit over time.
  • 6. Check Log tab — see exactly what happened each day.

⌨️ Keyboard Shortcuts

  • Enter / Space — Next Day
  • ↑ / ↓ arrows — Adjust quantity by ±5
  • 1 — Dashboard tab   2 — Costs tab   3 — Charts tab   4 — Log tab
